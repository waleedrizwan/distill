City council approves plan to restore the old river wetlands

By Staff Writer
Published 14 March 2025

The city council voted on Tuesday to restore more than 200 hectares of wetland along the lower river, reversing a drainage scheme that dates back to the 1950s. The plan will remove two kilometres of flood wall and allow the river to spill onto former pasture during high water.

Supporters say the wetlands will store floodwater that currently overwhelms the town centre roughly once every five years. Engineers estimate the restored marsh could hold about four million cubic metres of water, enough to lower peak flood levels downstream by up to forty centimetres.

READ MORE: Five things to know about the new flood maps .

The project will cost an estimated 18 million euros over six years, with most of the money coming from a regional climate adaptation fund. Farmers who currently lease the land will be offered alternative plots or compensation, and a grazing scheme will let some cattle return once the marsh has settled.

Not everyone is convinced. A group of residents near the old mill argued at the meeting that the higher water table could damage the foundations of older houses. The council agreed to fund a groundwater survey before any flood wall is removed.
